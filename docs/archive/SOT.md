# OUTBREAK

## Core Game System & Design Foundation

## 1. Game Concept

**OUTBREAK** is a competitive educational card game about the interaction between pathogens, hosts, immune systems, and medical interventions.

Players use real microorganisms, diseases, immune responses, treatments, and environmental factors to establish infections and trigger an **Outbreak** before their opponent can contain or eliminate them.

The game is designed primarily for **medical students and medically literate players**. Real microbiology, immunology, pharmacology, and epidemiology concepts should be reflected in the game's mechanics.

> **Core design principle: Biology is the game system.**
>
> Educational information should emerge naturally from gameplay rather than being added as disconnected trivia.

---

# 2. Core Gameplay

Each match represents a biological arms race between:

* Pathogens
* The Host
* The Immune System
* Medical Interventions
* Environmental Factors

Players deploy cards, establish infections, replicate pathogens, trigger immune responses, develop resistance or mutations, and attempt to control the progression of disease.

The primary gameplay loop is:

**Exposure → Infection → Replication → Immune Response → Progression → Outcome**

---

# 3. Primary Win Condition

The primary objective is to successfully establish and progress an infection until an **Outbreak Threshold** is reached.

Pathogens progress through stages:

1. **Exposure**
2. **Colonization / Infection**
3. **Replication**
4. **Disease Progression**
5. **Outbreak**

The opposing player attempts to:

1. Detect the pathogen
2. Activate immune defenses
3. Contain the infection
4. Administer treatment
5. Eliminate the pathogen

Alternative victory conditions may be introduced later for specific pathogens, such as latency, chronic infection, immune suppression, or host manipulation.

* Bloodstream

* Nervous System

# 8. Immune Response

Immune responses occur in stages.

## Innate Response

Immediate but nonspecific.

Innate cards can:

* Reduce pathogen populations
* Limit replication
* Trigger inflammation
* Damage infected cells
* Slow disease progression

---

## Adaptive Response

Slower to develop but highly specific.

Adaptive cards can:

* Target specific pathogens
* Generate antibodies
* Destroy infected cells
* Create immune memory

This creates a natural strategic progression:

**Early Defense → Delayed Specific Response → Immune Memory**

# 9. Treatments & Resistance

Medical treatments should interact with pathogen biology rather than acting as generic damage cards.

Examples:

### Antibiotic

Targets susceptible bacteria.

### Antiviral

Interferes with specific viral processes.

### Antifungal

Targets fungal-specific structures or processes.

### Vaccine

Prepares the immune system before or during future exposure.

---

## Antimicrobial Resistance

Pathogens may develop resistance through:

* Mutation
* Gene acquisition
* Selection pressure
* Previous exposure

Resistance should provide meaningful strategic advantages while reflecting real biological tradeoffs whenever possible.

Mechanisms may include:

* Antigenic Drift
* Antigenic Shift

# 11. Transmission

Pathogens spread according to real transmission mechanisms.

Transmission cards and abilities can allow pathogens to:

# 12. Disease Progression

* Tissue damage

# 15. Design Philosophy

OUTBREAK should prioritize:

### Fun

The game must work as a competitive card game before its educational value is considered.

### Biological Authenticity

Mechanics should be inspired by real microbiology whenever practical.

### Simplicity

The player should eventually understand concepts such as:

* Why antibiotics do not treat viruses
* How pathogens replicate
* How immune memory works
* Why resistance develops
* Why different pathogens target different tissues

# 18. Player Roles

## Core Concept

Each player represents a different force within the biological conflict.

The primary game mode is:

### Pathogen Player vs Host Defense Player

The two roles are intentionally asymmetrical.

The **Pathogen Player** attempts to establish, replicate, spread, and progress an infection.

The **Host Defense Player** attempts to detect, contain, diagnose, treat, and eliminate the infection.

This creates a strategic conflict based directly on real host-pathogen interactions.

# 26. Recommended Simplification

For the first playable prototype, use only **one shared resource system**.

Suggested name:

## Biological Energy

Players gain:

**1 Biological Energy at the start of each player's active phase.**

Reactive responses do not grant a second resource refresh. This keeps the
conversation moving without allowing a player to bank twice as much energy in
one exchange.

Cards cost Biological Energy to play.

This resource can later be replaced with a more thematic system once the core gameplay has been tested.

The first goal should be determining:

> Is establishing and defending against an infection fun?

Not:

> Is the resource system medically perfect?

# 32. Biological Conversation Turn Structure

Players do not take isolated turns in which one player acts while the other
waits. A round is a short biological conversation:

**Pathogen Turn -> Host Response -> Host Turn -> Pathogen Response**

The player taking the active turn may perform up to **2 actions**. The other
player receives one response window after each action and may play one legal
Response card or activate one Response ability. A response resolves
immediately, then the active player may continue or pass.

## Pathogen Turn

1. Pathogen player draws one card and gains one Biological Energy.
2. Resolve pathogen start-of-turn effects and automatic replication.
3. Perform up to two Pathogen actions, opening a Host response window after
	each action.
4. Resolve end-of-turn spread and other effects that specifically use the
	Pathogen turn.

## Host Response

The Host Defense player may respond to each Pathogen action. Examples include
deploying a Neutrophil after a bacterial population increases, using Isolation
after a Spread action, or revealing a diagnostic result after a new infection
is established.

## Host Turn

1. Host Defense player draws one card and gains one Biological Energy.
2. Resolve Host start-of-turn effects and ongoing treatments.
3. Perform up to two Host actions, opening a Pathogen response window after
	each action.
4. Resolve end-of-turn immune and clinical effects.

## Pathogen Response

The Pathogen player may respond to each Host action. Examples include using
Immune Evasion after an immune deployment, Mutating after a diagnostic test,
or activating a Resistance card after treatment is declared.

## Round Resolution

After both active phases and all response windows resolve:

1. Apply population, infection, tissue damage, and disease progression changes.
2. Check location thresholds and the Outbreak threshold.
3. Advance ongoing effects and discard temporary responses.

This sequence ensures that every meaningful action creates a biological
decision for the opponent. It also prevents a player from safely assembling
an entire board before the other player can intervene.

# 33. Card Anatomy

Every prototype card uses the same readable structure. The numbers describe
what the card changes; the rules text explains the biological exception.

```text
NAME
Card type | Class | Cost

INFECTIVITY  0-5   REPLICATION  0-5
VIRULENCE    0-5   PERSISTENCE  0-5

Transmission: route
Tropism: compatible host location or tissue
Timing: Action, Response, Ongoing, or Automatic
Effect: the immediate mechanical result
Vulnerability: the immune or treatment interaction that can answer it
Clinical fact: one concise educational fact
```

Only pathogen cards need all four pathogen stats. Immune, diagnostic,
treatment, and environment cards use the same header but replace irrelevant
stats with the relevant value, such as **Clearance**, **Specificity**, or
**Duration**. A card should normally have one primary effect and at most one
conditional effect so that the numbers remain testable.

---

# 34. Prototype Numerical Stat System

Use a 0-5 scale for the first simulations. A 0 means the property is absent
or unusable; 5 is exceptional. The values are not abstract power ratings:
each one feeds a specific part of the Host state.

## Infectivity

Infectivity measures how effectively a pathogen establishes infection when it
enters a compatible location.

When an Exposure or Infect action targets a compatible location, roll one d6.
The pathogen establishes infection if the roll is less than or equal to
**Infectivity + exposure modifiers**. On success, place one Infection and one
population. A location can only be infected when the pathogen's transmission
route and tropism are compatible, unless a card says otherwise.

## Replication

Replication measures the automatic population increase during the pathogen's
Biology step. An established pathogen adds **Replication** to its population,
up to the location's current capacity. Viruses require compatible infected
host cells; parasites and other pathogens may replace this with a lifecycle
condition printed on the card.

## Virulence

Virulence converts pathogen presence into clinical harm. During round
resolution, add **1 Disease** for every full **3 population**, then add 1
additional Disease when the pathogen's Virulence is 4 or 5. Toxin, immune
evasion, and tissue-damage effects may modify this conversion, but population
alone should not automatically equal severe disease.

## Persistence

Persistence measures how difficult established infection is to clear. When a
defense or treatment removes population, reduce the amount removed by
**Persistence - 1**, to a minimum of 0. A Persistence of 0 or 1 therefore
does not reduce ordinary clearance; a Persistence of 5 reduces each ordinary
clearance effect by 4.

## Infection and Disease Tracks

Each host location tracks Infection from 0-5. Successful establishment adds 1;
automatic replication adds 1 when population reaches 3, 6, or 9. At the end
of a round, Disease increases by the Virulence conversion above if Infection
is at least 2. Disease is a shared 0-10 Host track:

* 0-2: exposure or contained infection
* 3-5: active disease
* 6-8: severe disease
* 9-10: Outbreak; the Pathogen player wins

The Host Defense player wins when all active pathogen populations are 0 and
all locations have Infection 0 at round resolution. This gives both players a
clear race to measure during simulations.

## Stat Tradeoffs

Stats should create different biological identities rather than a single
power curve. During card design, a high value should usually carry a cost:

* High Infectivity with low Replication creates a fast but fragile infection.
* High Replication with low Infectivity creates a slow-growing established
  infection.
* High Virulence with low Persistence creates severe but treatable disease.
* High Persistence with low Virulence creates a chronic containment problem.

Mutation and resistance cards should alter a stat or rule while imposing a
visible tradeoff, such as **+1 Resistance / -1 Replication**.

# 35. Simulation Protocol

Before expanding the card pool, build ten real pathogen cards and simulate at
least five complete rounds for each matchup. Record after every response:

* Pathogen population by location
* Infection by location
* Host Disease
* Biological Energy spent and remaining
* Cards played and unspent response opportunities
* Whether either player had a meaningful choice

The first balance questions are:

1. Can the Host respond before a pathogen reaches a runaway state?
2. Do Infectivity, Replication, Virulence, and Persistence produce visibly
	different strategies?
3. Does the response window create decisions rather than constant interruption?
4. Can a correct diagnosis or treatment change the outcome without making
	the pathogen irrelevant?

If a stat does not change a decision in repeated simulations, remove it,
change its rule, or make the relevant interaction more visible before adding
more cards.

> How do I establish and maintain infection?

* Where do I infect?
* When do I replicate?
* Should I spread?
* Should I mutate?
* How do I evade immunity?
* Should I develop resistance?

---

Asks:

> What is causing this disease and how do I stop it?

Key decisions:

* What pathogen am I facing?
* Which diagnostic test should I use?
* Do I contain or treat?
* Which immune response is appropriate?
* Should I use broad or targeted treatment?
* Is resistance likely?
